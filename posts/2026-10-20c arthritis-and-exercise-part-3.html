Why you Should Exercise if you have Arthritis
by Susan Ingraham, BCRPA-certified fitness instructor for older adults

We know that regular, moderate physical activity has many benefits. As well, research shows that well-regulated exercise does not produce or aggravate joint symptoms. ***More specifically, some level of physical activity is necessary to preserve joint function.*** A combination of aerobic exercise, strength training, stretch and flexibility — taught by a qualified instructor — is the most effective. The primary goals of an exercise program for those suffering from arthritis are: * **Restore or maintain joint range of motion**: Moving the joints regularly is important to maintain flexibility so that daily activities can be done. * **Restore or maintain muscle strength**: Strengthening exercises can increase tone and functional strength of the muscles surrounding the joint, making it easier to move the joint. * **Interrupt the Chronic Pain Cycle**: When moving the joint is painful, there is a tendency to stop moving it. Continued disuse of the joint may cause stiffness and a shortening of the muscles and connective tissues around the joint (known as joint contracture). Put very simply: loss of movement leads to loss of function. * **Improve cardiorespiratory endurance**: Low-impact aerobic exercise is safe and desirable for individuals with arthritis. Not only does it contribute to one’s overall fitness level, it also will mean less joint stiffness. * **Improve posture and body awareness**: Posture may be improved through balance and coordination exercises. * **Relieve stiffness**: Regular exercise is critical in maintaining ease of movement. * **Maximize pulmonary function**: Breathing and chest expansion exercises encourage maximal lung function. * **Promote relaxation**: Any exercise program requires a relaxation period at the end. Relaxation techniques soothe the muscles and decrease stress. * **Enhance quality of life**: Exercise may improve self-image and self-esteem. Positive social interaction in a class may increase one’s feeling of self-worth and the positive feeling of knowing that you have the ability to take care of yourself.

Source: Exercise and Arthritis by Stephanie Harris, MD, and Gwen Hyatt, MS
