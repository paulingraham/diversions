“If a storyteller thinks enough of storytelling to regard it as a calling, unlike a historian he cannot turn from the sufferings of his characters. A storyteller, unlike a historian, must follow compassion wherever it leads him. He must be able to accompany his characters, even into smoke and fire, and bear witness to what they thought and felt even when they themselves no longer knew.”

Norman Maclean, Young Men and Fire

Union Cemetery, founded on November 9, 1857, is the oldest public cemetery in Kansas City, Missouri. In Section 56, lot 2, stand two monuments quite different in style and colour.
The Brand and Ham monuments, Union Cemetery

One stone is a large pink spire — at least ten feet tall — which seems to go on forever and is certainly taller than any other nearby monument. A mother and daughter are buried here together under one stone:

On one side:

Sarah E. Brand
Died January 27, 1890
Aged 36 yrs 9 mos 23 days
Jesus Lover of my Soul
Let me to thy bosom lie.

On the other side:

MOTHER

Sarah Ann Brand
Wife of
John Kell
Feb 5, 1831
Feb 29, 1904
Asleep in Jesus

Beside the mother-and-daughter Sarahs is another monument, a grey stone sarcophagus with large letters — HAM — on both sides. At one end, the inscription reads:

Julia H Brand Ham
Oct 20, 1862
Dec 6, 1927
Rest in Peace

Their deaths are separated by 37 years, but despite marriages or other relationships, they chose to lie here together. A mother and two of her daughters: Sarah Ann Brand, Sarah Etta Brand, and Julia Harriet Brand. Together for eternity, but they surely miss Clarinda.
